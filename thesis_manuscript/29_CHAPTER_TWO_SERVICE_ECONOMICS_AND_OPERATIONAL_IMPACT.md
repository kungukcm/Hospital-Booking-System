# CHAPTER 2 SUPPLEMENT E. SERVICE ECONOMICS AND OPERATIONAL IMPACT

## 1. Introduction

This supplement extends the literature review by focusing on the service economics of chatbot-enabled appointment systems. It explores how administrative automation affects cost, throughput, workload, and perceived service value.

## 2. Administrative Cost as a Hidden Burden

Hospitals incur significant hidden costs in administrative coordination. Staff spend time answering repetitive scheduling questions that follow predictable patterns. Time is lost to repeated clarification of dates, services, and patient details when the system for capturing these is inefficient. Inefficiencies arise from manual rewriting or re-entry of information, creating error opportunities and wasting staff attention. Delays are caused by congestion in phone or desk-based scheduling channels during peak hours. Additionally, opportunity costs occur when staff attention is pulled away from higher-value tasks like patient education or complex problem-solving. Chatbots do not eliminate these costs entirely, but they can shift or reduce them by taking over repetitive communication tasks and structuring the booking process.

## 3. Throughput and Queue Economics

Queue economics provides a useful lens for this thesis. In high-demand systems, a few time slots can become overloaded while others remain underused. If users select times without guidance, demand may cluster inefficiently.

Queue-aware recommendations address this by making lower-congestion periods visible. The likely operational effect is more balanced demand distribution, which can support smoother staff workload and better use of available service windows.

The relevant economic benefit is not merely speed; it is a more efficient alignment between demand and service capacity.

## 4. Patient Search Costs

In service economics, search cost refers to the effort required to find available options. A chatbot reduces search cost by answering questions in natural language and presenting ranked options directly. This matters in hospital settings because patients may otherwise need to call multiple times, wait in a queue, or ask staff repeatedly for availability.

The assistant therefore acts as a search-cost reducer. It gives the user a shorter path from intent to viable booking choice.

## 5. Cost of Miscommunication

Miscommunication in scheduling is expensive and can cascade into cascading problems. Missed appointments occur when patients misunderstand the date or time of their booking. Patient frustration rises when communication failures create doubt about booking status. Administrative rework becomes necessary when bookings must be canceled or corrected due to misunderstanding. Unnecessary travel happens when patients arrive at wrong times or facilities based on confusion. Delayed care access results when misunderstood bookings push patients further back in queues. The thesis argues that clarity improvements are not merely user-experience enhancements but direct cost-reduction mechanisms that benefit both institutions and patients.

## 9. Resource-Constrained Optimization

In resource-constrained environments, systems should not attempt to automate everything at once. Instead, they should target high-volume, repetitive administrative tasks with clear value.

The assistant fits that logic because booking and scheduling are frequent, routine, and structurally suitable for hybrid automation.
