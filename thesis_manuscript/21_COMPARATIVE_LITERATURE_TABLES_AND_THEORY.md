# CHAPTER 2 SUPPLEMENT C. COMPARATIVE LITERATURE TABLES AND THEORY ALIGNMENT

## 1. Introduction

This supplement converts the literature review into a comparison-oriented analytical resource. It highlights what the literature says, how the sources differ, and what each difference means for the present study.

## 2. Comparative Dimensions

The literature supplied in the project repository can be compared along the following dimensions:

1. domain focus;
2. interaction style;
3. primary benefit claim;
4. main limitation or risk;
5. governance relevance;
6. direct contribution to this thesis.

## 4. Theory Alignment Table

### 4.1 Socio-Technical Systems Theory

This thesis aligns strongly with socio-technical systems theory because the artifact is shaped by human workflows, institutional constraints, language preferences, and technical components simultaneously.

The system is not designed to replace staff but to reconfigure service interactions around a more efficient and reliable support structure.

### 4.2 Design Science Research Theory

The study’s artifact-centered logic aligns naturally with design science. Knowledge contribution arises through a built and evaluated solution rather than a purely descriptive account.

### 4.3 Technology Acceptance Logic

Although not formalized as a full acceptance-model study, the thesis echoes acceptance constructs:

1. perceived usefulness via queue guidance;
2. perceived ease of use via conversational access;
3. trust via consistent booking completion.

### 4.4 Public Value Theory

The work also reflects public value thinking: the assistant should improve service access, fairness, and transparency in a publicly relevant setting.

## 5. Gaps and Tensions Across the Literature

### 5.1 Capability vs Control

The literature often celebrates chatbot flexibility but underestimates the need for action control in transactional contexts. This thesis resolves that tension with deterministic gating.

### 5.2 Accessibility vs Safety

Broader access can increase exposure to incorrect advice if safety boundaries are weak. The thesis avoids this by restricting scope to administrative tasks.

### 5.3 Personalization vs Privacy

Personalized interactions improve engagement but may increase data exposure. The thesis recommends data minimization and clear governance controls.

## 6. Why the Present Artifact Is Distinct

Compared with the literature, the present artifact is distinctive because it integrates all of the following:

1. multilingual transactional consistency;
2. queue-aware appointment recommendations;
3. deterministic booking-state controls;
4. structured conflict detection and recovery;
5. policy-oriented deployment guidance.

This combination is rare in the supplied literature and forms the core research contribution.

## 7. Literature-Driven Design Claims

The thesis makes six literature-grounded claims:

1. administrative healthcare chatbots should be bounded by explicit role definitions;
2. booking flows need deterministic validation at action points;
3. queue-aware guidance improves informed selection;
4. language consistency must extend to final confirmations;
5. evaluation should include completion and recovery metrics;
6. governance readiness is part of implementation quality.

## 8. Summary

This comparative synthesis shows that the literature is broadly supportive of chatbot-based health support but also reveals unresolved reliability and governance gaps. The artifact developed in this thesis directly addresses those gaps through a hybrid, policy-aware design.
