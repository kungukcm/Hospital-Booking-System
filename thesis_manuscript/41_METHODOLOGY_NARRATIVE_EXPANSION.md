# CHAPTER 3 SUPPLEMENT E. NARRATIVE EXPANSION OF METHOD, PROCESS, AND ANALYTICAL LOGIC

This supplement provides a more continuous narrative account of the methodological choices made in the study. The aim is to explain the design science process in a way that reads like a thesis chapter rather than a checklist of research steps. The study began from a practical service problem rather than from a purely theoretical question. Appointment scheduling in hospital environments was identified as a process that affects access, waiting time, staff burden, and patient confidence. Because the problem was operational and applied, a design science approach was appropriate: the research needed to create a solution, test it, and learn from its behavior.

The research process was iterative. Early versions of the artifact focused on basic booking flow and natural language interpretation. Those versions were useful as a proof of concept, but they also revealed that a conversational assistant in a healthcare setting cannot rely on language understanding alone. The system had to be shaped so that it could ask for missing details, preserve state across turns, and resist incomplete or ambiguous inputs. As the work progressed, the method became more disciplined. Each observed failure became an input into the next design revision, which is exactly the kind of learning cycle design science is meant to support.

The artifact evaluation logic was also shaped by practical constraints. Instead of relying only on abstract quality claims, the evaluation focused on whether the assistant could complete booking flows under realistic conversational conditions. This meant testing how the system responded when users gave only a service type, only a date, only a time, or only partial patient details. These scenarios were chosen because they represent common human behavior in chat-based interactions. Real users rarely provide information in the tidy sequence expected by a form-based interface, so the system needed to demonstrate resilience to conversational incompleteness.

A further methodological choice concerned language behavior. Since the project explicitly supports English and Swahili, the evaluation had to look not only at whether the assistant could translate at a surface level but whether it could preserve the correct language context at the transactional stage. This distinction matters because an assistant can appear multilingual during casual conversation while still reverting to a default language at the confirmation point. The study therefore treated language consistency as part of the evaluation method, not just as an implementation detail.

Validation was conducted in a layered manner. First, the internal parsing logic was checked to confirm that appointment types, dates, and times were interpreted correctly. Second, the flow-control logic was checked to ensure that the assistant moved through the proper booking stages without skipping mandatory steps. Third, the tool execution logic was checked to confirm that only valid and complete booking requests were sent into the appointment system. Finally, the output layer was checked to ensure that confirmations and slot recommendations were intelligible and contextually aligned with the user’s language.

The method also reflected research integrity concerns. Because the project is implementation-driven, it was important not to overstate what the system could do. The thesis therefore maintained a clear boundary between administrative assistance and clinical advice. This boundary is not simply a safety precaution; it is part of the research design itself. By keeping the assistant focused on scheduling and queue guidance, the study remained within a manageable scope that could be validated more rigorously.

The result of this methodological approach is a research process that is both technically traceable and thesis-appropriate. The chapter supplements in this package preserve the structure of the study while presenting the logic in a way that is easier to read as continuous scholarly prose. The main methodological point is that the artifact was not just built; it was built through repeated correction, validation, and scope refinement. That iterative discipline is what makes the final system credible as a research output.
